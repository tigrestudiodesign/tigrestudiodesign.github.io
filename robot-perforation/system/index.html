UNIT
SYSTEM

One system for a fleet of tracked utility humanoids: the screen on the robot's chest, the decal on its base, the operator console, the booth wall and the sales deck. Tokens, mark, icons, components, patterns and the rules that hold them together.

00

PRINCIPLES

Four rules decide every argument the specs don't cover. If a design breaks one, it doesn't ship.

P1

Say what it does

Plain words, real numbers, no hype. If we can't measure it, we don't claim it.

P2

Built for the floor

Everything works at 6 metres, under warehouse light, with gloves on. Pretty comes second.

P3

Safety is not a style

Red, yellow and green have one job each. They never decorate, never carry a brand moment.

P4

One family, every unit

Every robot reads as the same brand and still can be told apart. The mark does both.

01

COLOUR

Two ramps carry the brand, three safety colours carry meaning. Primitives are never used directly in product: designers pick a role, and the role points at a primitive. Click any swatch to copy its hex.

Roles

Contrast is calculated live in your browser against the role's intended ground (WCAG 2.x). Anything below 4.5:1 is restricted to large text or graphics, and the table says so.

RoleTokenPoints toOnContrast
RuleShell teal (teal-500) never carries text on paper: it only reaches 2.8:1. Teal text uses fg-brand (teal-800, 6.5:1). Teal-500 is for fills, the chevron and graphics.

Colour by area

Paper 44 · Ink 26 · Shell teal 18 · Steel 8 · Safety 4, and only where something is actually safe, running or stopped

02

TYPE

Two families, seven styles. Inter Display Black says what a thing is; JetBrains Mono says what it is doing. Headlines are always uppercase; body copy never is.

Why monoStatus, zones, unit numbers and timestamps are machine data. Setting them in mono makes them scan like a readout and keeps digits from jumping when they change.
03

SPACE · GRID · RADIUS

The base unit is 8 px, taken from the idea of the perforation on the robot's shell. Every gap, margin and radius comes from it, so screens and hardware share one rhythm.

Space scale
8 px perforation pitch
Layout grid

12 columns, 24 px gutters on desktop. 4 columns, 16 px on mobile. Turn it on and check this page against it.

Radius

TileThe mark's corner (4.2 of 32 cells, 13.125%) was chosen to echo the rounded chassis panels. Use radius-tile only for the mark and app icons.
04

MARK

A 32 × 32 perforated tile: 1,024 positions, 992 holes once the rounded corners are cut. The chevron of full-size holes never changes. The field is generated from the unit number, so each robot carries its own mark. The full guidelines, lockups and misuse sheet are on the case study.

05

ICONS

Eighteen icons drawn for the floor, not the app store. 24 px grid, 2 px stroke, square caps, 45° and 90° only: the same geometry as the chevron. Click one to copy its SVG.

Construction

Live area 20 × 20 inside a 2 px pad. Stroke 2 px at 24, scaled, never thinned. Square caps and mitre joins so icons sit next to the chevron without looking softer.

TEMPLATES

09

MOTION

The robot moves deliberately, so the brand does too: fast in, firm stop, nothing bounces. Four durations, three curves. With reduced motion on, every duration drops to zero.

10

ACCESSIBILITY

WCAG 2.2 AA is the floor, not the goal. This matrix is computed in the page from the tokens, so it can't drift from them. The whole site is audited with axe-core: no violations, apart from the failing cells in this matrix, which fail on purpose.

Never colour alone

Every status has an icon and a word. It reads in greyscale and to colour-blind operators.

44 px targets

Gloves, tablets on carts, shaking hands. Nothing tappable is smaller.

Visible focus

3 px teal-800 ring, 2 px offset, on every interactive part. Tab through this page.

Motion off

Reduced motion stops pulses, sweeps and the console animation steps instead.

12

GOVERNANCE

A system is only as good as the way it changes. Here's how a request becomes a token, and the checklist every piece passes before it goes out the door: the brand guardian's job, written down.

01Request

Anyone files a need, not a solution: "operators can't see low battery from 6 m."

02Review

Brand checks it against the principles and the existing parts. Most requests are solved with what exists.

03Build

New tokens or components land in tokens.json first, then in code, then in the asset library.

04Release

Semantic version, changelog, a short note to every team that uses the part.
