OHM-01

ZERO RESISTANCE.

Om, the breath. Ohm, the unit of resistance. A women's puffer high-top built like soft hardware: tuned with a heel dial, cinched with a bungee, gripped by a parametric lug sole. Cosy enough for the studio, ready for a short trail.

HOW THESE WERE MADE

MODEL FIRST, THEN RENDER

The shape, the perforation pattern, the lockup and the colour blocking all come from the Python + Blender model in rounds 01–07. That hero render went into Vizcom (Instant Render) with a written material spec: matte ripstop with stitched baffles, knit collar, soft-touch TPU counter, EVA foam, light grey studio sweep. Four options came back and these two were kept.

What the AI pass changed: fabric realism, studio light and the knit collar. What it simplified: the dial reads as a generic knob, and the small lululemon type softens.

ANATOMY

What every part is doing
01 / COLLAR

PADDED, GATHERED OPENING

A tilted oval, 32 cm around and lower at the front so the foot slides in. The ripstop roll is gathered by elastic, with microfleece inside.

02 / CINCH

BUNGEE + CORD LOCK

A second gathered channel lower on the shaft. A braided bungee exits at the instep into a matte nylon cord lock.

03 / FIT

HEEL TUNE DIAL

A knurled, radial-brushed anodised dial on a coloured anodised bezel. One twist sets heel hold.

04 / UPPER

RIPSTOP PUFFER

4.5 mm box ripstop with a satin DWR coat, stitched into baffles with soft wrinkles, and lined in microfleece.

05 / COUNTER

SOFT-TOUCH HEEL CUP

Matte stipple TPU with a rolled lip, a raised satin-ink OHM lockup and a woven pull loop.

06 / MIDSOLE

SPECKLED, PERFORATED EVA

393 holes on a hex grid, big under the heel and small at the toe, down to a gloss TPU core. Colour-matched flecks run through the foam.

07 / OUTSOLE

PARAMETRIC HIKE LUGS

Heel lugs brake, forefoot lugs propel, edge lugs grip, and two flex grooves let it bend. Topographic micro-texture throughout.

08 / TOE

DIAMOND BUMPER

A textured TPU toe cap with a diamond emboss that resists scuffs and echoes the knurled dial.

THE DESIGN PROCESS

Ten rounds, each one a response
  1. 01 BRIEFRead the ask: who it's for, the brand DNA to keep, what "techy" should mean.
  2. 02 MAPTranslate each reference into a shoe part, so it inspires the design without copying it.
  3. 03 FORMSilhouette and proportion as signed distance fields: fast, smooth and fully editable.
  4. 04 ENGINEEREntry, fit, flex and traction set as numbers, checked against real products.
  5. 05 CMFEvery material gets its own colour, roughness, sheen and surface relief.
  6. 06 VALIDATESection plots, macro crops and measurements before each final render.
  7. 07 TELLRenders, specs, maps and this page.

  1. ROUND 01

    LOW-TOP “SIGNAL”

    Take the electronics literally

    Feedback
    Brief: a comfortable, cool, techy lululemon women’s shoe inspired by modern electronics.
    Diagnosis
    No form yet. The risk was a generic sneaker with gadget stickers on it.
    Decisions
    • Mapped each reference part to a shoe part: synth knobs → tune dial, chassis cut-outs → heel frame, speaker grilles → vents, patch cables → lacing.
    • Kept the lululemon DNA: a quilted upper, a rolled collar and a soft one-piece midsole.
    • Three colourways (Signal, SR-01, Module), each drawn from a different reference.
    Parameters
    • Model: signed distance fields (SDF), meshed at 0.6 mm
    • Dial: 21 tick marks on a 0–10 scale
    Result
    It looked like a device, not a shoe she would wear. Too many parts competing.
  2. ROUND 02

    HIGH-TOP, PUNCHIER

    Fewer, bigger moves

    Feedback
    Make it a high-top, less techy, more punchy. Then: remove the air-like chambers and use a Voronoi pattern instead.
    Diagnosis
    Punch comes from scale and contrast, not part count. The window cut-outs read as a visible-air cushioning unit, which is another brand’s language.
    Decisions
    • Raised the shaft into a quilted high-top and removed the cable, screws, grilles and micro-type.
    • Kept one hero detail, an oversized dial, plus bold colour blocks.
    • A 3D Voronoi pattern cut into the midsole, with the core colour showing in the grooves.
    Parameters
    • Shaft top: 14.4 cm at the heel
    • Voronoi cell size: 1.85 cm
    Result
    Much bolder. The Voronoi read as computational, but later as crude.
  3. ROUND 03

    +25% CMF & MODELLING

    Make materials believable

    Feedback
    Improve the CMF and the modelling by 25%.
    Diagnosis
    The surfaces were plastic-smooth and the logo was garbled 3D text.
    Decisions
    • Stitch lines and soft wrinkles on every puff, plus a rolled lip on the counter.
    • Voronoi graded from small at the heel to large at the toe, and domed (pillowed).
    • Satin ripstop, fleece lining, soft-touch TPU, foam pores, a candy core and an anodised dial.
    • Logo moved from 3D text to a printed decal.
    Parameters
    • Mesh: 0.6 → 0.5 mm
    • Stitch groove: 0.5 mm deep
    Result
    It read as real fabric and foam. The logo was finally clean.
  4. ROUND 04

    SOLE REFINED

    From cracked mud to moulded

    Feedback
    The sole looks crude. Refine the texture. (Material samples attached.)
    Diagnosis
    At shoe scale the Voronoi read as dried mud. Every sample you sent showed a graded, ordered pattern.
    Decisions
    • A hex field of round perforations, graded from big at the heel to small at the toe and fading at the edges, after the perforated helmet sample.
    • Each hole cuts down to the coloured core, with a filleted rim.
    • Finer foam pore and a topographic line texture on the rubber.
    Parameters
    • 524 holes, radius 0.6–2.45 mm
    • Hole depth: 5.5 mm, rim fillet: 0.35 mm
    Result
    Clean, moulded and parametric. One defect left at the heel.
  5. ROUND 04.1

    HEEL FIX

    Debug the geometry, not the render

    Feedback
    The finish at the heel looks weird.
    Diagnosis
    Each row of holes starts and ends at the heel, so two sets of holes landed on top of each other there and fused into a blob. The colour edge was set per mesh point, which made it ragged.
    Decisions
    • Overlap pruning: no two holes closer than the sum of their radii plus a 0.7 mm wall.
    • Wider hex spacing so the pattern stays regular.
    • The colour edge became an exact curve in the shader. Midsole mesh 35% finer.
    Parameters
    • Hole pitch: 5 → 6 mm, 524 → 393 holes, radius 0.6–2.0 mm
    • Midsole mesh: 0.32 mm
    Result
    The heel finish is clean and the colour line is crisp.
  6. ROUND 05

    OUTSOLE + LOCKUP

    Design the sole for a short hike

    Feedback
    Fix foot entry and the OHM branding. Make the outsole parametric, for a short hike.
    Diagnosis
    The waveform bars were graphic, not functional. Warped 3D letters read as broken.
    Decisions
    • Rules for the lug field: triangle lugs on a hex grid. Heel lugs point forward to brake, forefoot lugs point back to propel.
    • Edge lugs about 30% larger and angled outward for grip on uneven ground. Two flex grooves.
    • An “OHM / lululemon” lockup printed onto the counter.
    Parameters
    • Lug pitch: 11.2 mm, height 4.2 mm (2.8 mm under the arch)
    • Lug taper: 14% toward the tip, for mud release
    Result
    The sole now does a job. Entry was still not solved.
  7. ROUND 06

    THE COLLAR, RESEARCHED

    Build it the way booties are built

    Feedback
    Really fix the collar. Research this type of shoe.
    Diagnosis
    Research on puffer booties: an elastic gathered opening, often a second gather lower down, visible fleece lining, a drawcord with a toggle and a pull loop. Leg openings run about 28–34 cm. The model’s “flare” had the wrong sign, so the shaft narrowed toward the ankle.
    Decisions
    • Flipped the flare so the shaft widens upward, to 7.7 cm at the top.
    • Cut the opening with a plane tilted 11°, lower at the front for entry.
    • A padded roll gathered into about 44 fine pleats, a cinch channel, a bungee with cord lock, and a sewn-in pull loop.
    Parameters
    • Opening: 32 cm around (spec 28–34 cm)
    • Collar roll: 12 × 16 mm
    Result
    Entry works and it reads as a real bootie. The shaft is still a bit low.
  8. ROUND 07

    PAIR + COLOURWAYS

    Left, right, three moods

    Feedback
    Make a left and right pair in Punch, Obsidian and Manga.
    Diagnosis
    Mirroring a shoe also mirrors its print, so the lockup would read backwards on the right shoe.
    Decisions
    • The right shoe is a true mirror of the left, with its own mirrored print so the lockup reads correctly.
    • Each colourway is a small table of values in the code.
    Parameters
    • 3 colourways × 17 components
    • Pair stagger: 3.2 cm forward, 13° between the shoes
    Result
    A shoppable pair in three moods.
  9. ROUND 08

    VIZCOM RENDER PASS

    From CG to product photo

    Feedback
    Make the renders read like real product photography.
    Diagnosis
    The Round 07 hero was strong as a design, but it still read as CG.
    Decisions
    • Took the Round 07 PUNCH hero into Vizcom and rendered it against a written CMF spec, not a style word.
    • Kept the outputs that held the design: same silhouette, lockup and colour blocking, with real ripstop sheen, stitched baffles and a ribbed knit collar.
    Parameters
    • 4 options generated, 2 kept
    • Input: Round 07 PUNCH hero + written material spec
    Result
    The model sets the design; the AI pass only sells the materials. Anything it invents (the knit collar) is a question for the next round, not a decision.
  10. ROUND 09

    CMF +25%, MAPPED

    Every material earns its texture, in the model itself

    Feedback
    Improve CMF on every component by 25%, list every colour option and show the material maps.
    Diagnosis
    Several materials only differed by colour. The Vizcom pass showed what real materials add, so this round builds that into the model itself, where it stays exact and editable: fleece, foam, rubber and TPU each get their own texture, roughness and sheen.
    Decisions
    • Ripstop: a real yarn grid with soft yarn profiles, a micro-weave and a satin DWR coat.
    • Fleece: full sheen and a raised fuzz. Foam: speckled recycled-EVA look with roughness variation.
    • TPU: soft-touch stipple on the counter, diamond emboss on the toe, gloss translucent core.
    • Rubber: topographic micro-texture. Hardware: brushed and coloured anodising. Cord: braid.
    • Baked base colour, roughness and normal maps for every textured material, in all three colourways.
    Parameters
    • 10 materials, 27 baked maps per colourway
    • Fleck scale: 1.7 mm cells, 2 sizes
    Result
    Each material is now identifiable even in a close crop. Maps are below.
  11. ROUND 10

    VIZCOM ACROSS THE RANGE

    Same model, every colourway, a written CMF spec

    Feedback
    Improve the project using the Vizcom workflow on the CMF and the final renders.
    Diagnosis
    Round 08 only took PUNCH through Vizcom, so the three moods were judged in two different renderers. Round 09's maps made every material exact in the model; the photographic read still stopped at CG.
    Decisions
    • Each Round 07 pair render went into Vizcom (Pro) with the colourway's CMF table rewritten as a prompt: material, finish and colour for every part, plus one studio setup.
    • Fixed the collar to "same ripstop, gathered", so the model's construction holds instead of an invented knit.
    • A macro pass on the heel to test the CMF at the scale a buyer checks: ripstop grid, stipple, knurl, foam pores.
    • Picked by fidelity to the model, not by drama: shape, lockup and colour blocking must match the Blender frame.
    Parameters
    • 4 passes × 4 outputs, 4 kept
    • Input: Round 07 pairs + heel macro · output 2752 px
    Result
    All three moods now read as product photography, and the model stays the source of truth: switch any colourway back to BLENDER MODEL above.

WHAT I'D CARRY INTO THE NEXT SHOE

  • Check the maths before the materials. The collar problem was a sign error, not a modelling problem.
  • Fix the geometry, not the render. The heel blob was overlapping holes, and no shader would hide it.
  • Fewer, bigger moves read as confident. Round 2 deleted more than it added.
  • Research the category early. The bootie construction specs would have saved two rounds.

Next: raise the shaft about 2.5 cm (now about 3.5" above the footbed; real booties run 4.5–6.5"), and add a knit-collar variant.

THE PIPELINE

No viewport. Every change is code.

SIGNED DISTANCE FIELDS

Every part is a function in Python: footprint, last profile, puffs, collar, counter, perforation and lugs, joined with smooth blends.

MARCHING CUBES

Fields are meshed with scikit-image at 0.5 mm voxels, plus a 0.32 mm midsole pass. A KD-tree keeps the 393 hole cuts fast.

RULES AS PARAMETERS

Hole grading, lug direction, edge growth, flex grooves and the 32 cm opening are all numbers you can tune and re-run.

PROCEDURAL CMF

Materials are built from Blender shader nodes at real scale, in millimetres, and then baked to base colour, roughness and normal maps.

HEADLESS CYCLES

bpy builds the scene, studio and cameras from a script. Cycles renders with AgX Punchy and denoising.

COLOURWAYS AS DATA

Each colourway is a small table of hex values. The site's spec tables are generated from that same table.
